How this is computed

The algorithms, the simulations and where the data comes fromMETHODS
Destination scoring DETERMINISTIC

Every candidate hospital is scored out of 100 on six weighted factors the moment a case opens, and re-scored whenever live capacity changes. Nothing here is random — the same inputs always produce the same ranking, which is what makes a routing decision auditable.

clin 30 speciality matches the patient's need bed 25 ICU/general beds actually free right now eta 20 clamp(20 − (minutes − 5) × 1.05, 4, 20) load 10 (1 − ED load) × 10 conf 10 feed freshness × 10 ins 5 in-network — neutralised when severity = Critical ─── total 100 → highest score wins the reservation

Three rules override the arithmetic. A hospital reporting zero of the required bed type is struck out as infeasible regardless of score. A missing capacity feed scores a cautious 8/25 rather than being trusted or discarded outright. And when severity is Critical, network compatibility is scored equally for every candidate — so insurance can never decide where a life-threatening case is sent, only where a stable transfer goes.

Why weights, not distance DESIGN CHOICE

Nearest-first routing optimises a single variable that the ambulance can already see. The weighting exists so that clinical capability (30) plus real bed availability (25) together outweigh travel time (20) — a hospital 6 minutes further away wins if it can actually treat the patient on arrival.

The loop on the right is the part that matters: a reservation is not final. Any change in live capacity re-enters the same scoring function, and if the ranking flips the ambulance is rerouted mid-journey.

Three different simulations, three different jobs

1 · Live case SCRIPTED
A discrete-event timeline. Each scenario (S1–S8) is a fixed script of timed events — a decline at t+86s, a bed lost at t+180s — so a specific failure mode can be demonstrated on demand. The routing response to those events is not scripted: it is the live scoring function reacting.

2 · Side-by-side CONTROLLED
The same emergency is run twice against two policies — nearest-first versus capability-aware — holding the patient, hospitals and road conditions identical. It isolates the effect of the coordination layer alone.

3 · National model MONTE CARLO
Per state, N synthetic emergencies are drawn from normal distributions (Box–Muller), each timed end to end under both policies. Outputs are proportions, not individual predictions.

What is modelled DERIVED

Travel, response and notification times; refusal probability as a function of bed density; golden-hour rates under each policy; deaths averted. These are projections produced by the assumptions above, adjustable by the sliders on the national tab — not measured outcomes, and not a forecast.

What is illustrative SYNTHETIC

Every patient, hospital, operator, phone number, insurance policy and district road layout in the live console is invented for demonstration. Hospital capacity feeds, the cloud adapter and the event store are mocks — no real facility is represented and no credentials are used.

Known limits HONEST CAVEATS

The model claims no benefit on the crash-to-call leg, which is where a large share of deaths occur. It assumes capacity data can be made available in near-real time, which is the central adoption risk. State-level averages hide district variation. Elasticity between arrival time and survival is the single most sensitive assumption in the whole model — which is why it is exposed as a slider rather than fixed.
